## ⚠️ SAFETY WARNINGS - READ FIRST

### DO NOT USE TENS IF YOU HAVE

- ❌ Pacemaker or implanted electronic device
- ❌ Heart condition or arrhythmia  
- ❌ Epilepsy or seizure disorder
- ❌ Pregnancy
- ❌ Skin conditions or wounds in electrode area

### NEVER

- ❌ Place electrodes across the heart (chest)
- ❌ Place electrodes on the front or sides of the neck (carotid sinus area)
- ❌ **Place the return/ground pad on the RIGHT side of the neck, right shoulder, or anywhere on the right side of the body.** The active electrode is on the LEFT ear — the return MUST also be on the left side. Routing current from the left ear to the right side of the body forces it to cross the midline, passing through the brainstem, heart, and central nervous system. This is a serious safety hazard.
- ❌ Place electrodes on broken skin
- ❌ Use while driving or operating machinery
- ❌ Use while sleeping
- ❌ Exceed pain threshold - if uncomfortable, turn down immediately

> ### 🚨 CRITICAL: KEEP THE CURRENT ON ONE SIDE
>
> TENS delivers current between two electrodes. Whatever path the current takes between them is the tissue being stimulated. For this protocol:
>
> - **Active electrode:** left ear (cymba conchae or tragus)
> - **Return/ground pad:** lower-left neck or left trapezius/shoulder ONLY
>
> **Both contacts stay on the LEFT side of the body. Never on the right.** Crossing the midline routes current through the brainstem and CNS — do not do this.

### ALWAYS

- ✅ Start at intensity 0 and increase slowly
- ✅ Stop if you experience pain, dizziness, or discomfort
- ✅ Consult healthcare provider before starting
- ✅ Keep TENS unit away from water
- ✅ Have a trusted person nearby for first few sessions

**This is experimental. Not medical advice. Use at your own risk.**

## 📍 Electrode Placement for tVNS

### The Vagus Nerve Target

The **Vagus Nerve** has branches that reach the ear. Stimulating these activates the parasympathetic nervous system (rest-and-digest).

### Which Ear?

**Use the LEFT ear only.**

- The left ear has the stronger auricular vagal connection, which is the target of this protocol
- A single-channel setup on the left ear is sufficient — you do not need both ears
- Do not stimulate the right ear (different autonomic innervation; not the target of this protocol)

### Placement Options (Left Ear)

You have two acceptable clip locations on the left ear: the **cymba conchae** or the **tragus**. Both are validated tVNS targets. Pick based on comfort and whether you're using headphones.

#### Option 1: Cymba Conchae

**Location:** The small hollow depression just above the ear canal opening

```text
        Helix (outer rim)
              ↓
    ╔═══════════╗
    ║           ║
    ║  [●]←─────╫─── Cymba Conchae (PLACE ELECTRODE HERE)
    ║   ║       ║
    ║   ║  ●    ║
    ║   ╚══○════╣
    ║      \    ║
    ╚═══════\═══╝
             \
        Ear canal
```

**How to find it:**

1. Put finger in ear canal
2. Move up toward top of head about 1cm
3. You'll feel a small hollow - that's it
4. Should be comfortable, not painful

#### Option 2: Tragus

**Location:** The small triangular flap of cartilage that covers the ear canal opening

- Easier to clip onto
- More comfortable for some users
- A solid alternative when not wearing in-ear audio equipment

### Which placement should you choose?

- **If you are using earbuds or in-ear headphones with the bilateral audio: you MUST clip the cymba conchae.** The tragus sits directly over the ear canal and the clip will either block or be pushed out by the earbud. The cymba is tucked higher up and out of the way.
- **If you are using over-ear headphones or no audio at all:** either the cymba conchae or the tragus works. Pick whichever is more comfortable for you.

### 🚨 Return/Ground Pad Placement — Left Side ONLY

Every TENS channel has two leads: the active electrode (on your left ear) **and a return pad**. Where you put that return pad matters enormously.

**Correct placement for the return pad:**

- ✅ Lower-LEFT side of the neck (below the jawline, behind the left ear, back of the neck on the left)
- ✅ LEFT trapezius / top of the left shoulder
- ✅ Anywhere on the LEFT side of the body near the ear

**NEVER place the return pad:**

- ❌ Right side of the neck
- ❌ Right shoulder, right arm, or anywhere on the right side of the body
- ❌ Front of the throat (carotid sinus — causes blood-pressure drops)
- ❌ Across the chest

**Why this matters:** TENS current flows between the two electrodes. If one is on your left ear and the other is on the right side of your body, the current must cross the midline — directly through your brainstem, heart, and central nervous system. This is how you short-circuit autonomic function. **Keep both contacts on the LEFT side of your body, always.**

## ⚡ TENS Settings

### Initial Settings (Start Here)

- **Mode:** Normal/Continuous (not burst or modulated)
- **Frequency:** 25 Hz (if adjustable)
- **Pulse Width:** 200-250 microseconds (if adjustable)
- **Intensity:** START AT ZERO

### Finding Your Intensity

1. **Start at 0** with electrodes in place
2. **Slowly increase** by 1-2 levels at a time
3. **Stop when you feel:**
   - Gentle tingling/buzzing in the ear
   - Slight tickling sensation
   - Comfortable "presence" of stimulation
4. **Back off if you feel:**
   - Pain or sharp sensation
   - Jaw clenching
   - Dizziness or nausea
   - Discomfort

**Target intensity:** Just above perception threshold. You should barely notice it, not be bothered by it.

**Typical range:** 3-7 on most TENS units (varies by device)

## 🔄 The Manual Protocol: Step-by-Step

### Session Setup (5 minutes)

1. **Choose your space**
   - Quiet, private room
   - Comfortable seated position (or lying down)
   - Ensure you won't be interrupted for 30-60 minutes

2. **Attach electrodes**
   - Active electrode: LEFT ear only — cymba conchae or tragus (cymba required if using earbuds)
   - Return/ground pad: lower-LEFT neck or left shoulder — **never the right side of the body** (crossing the midline routes current through the brainstem/CNS)
   - Ensure good contact (moisten if using stick pads)
   - Turn TENS unit ON but intensity at ZERO

3. **Put on headphones** (if using)
   - Start bilateral audio track
   - Adjust to comfortable volume

4. **Baseline check**
   - Close eyes
   - Notice your current state
   - Heart rate, breathing, muscle tension
   - Emotional tone (calm, anxious, numb, etc.)

### Phase 1: Establish Baseline (5-10 minutes)

**Goal:** Experience what "regulated" feels like

1. **Turn on tVNS**
   - Slowly increase TENS intensity to comfortable level
   - Let it run for 5-10 minutes
   - Just breathe, no trauma work yet

2. **Notice the shift**
   - Breathing may slow and deepen
   - Shoulders/jaw may relax
   - Heart rate may decrease
   - Mental chatter may quiet
   - You might sigh or yawn (good sign!)

3. **This is your baseline**
   - Memorize this feeling
   - This is "ventral vagal" - parasympathetic activation
   - Safe, grounded, present

**Important:** Don't rush this. Spend 10+ minutes just experiencing regulation before trauma work.

### Phase 2: Trauma Targeting (Repeating Cycles)

Now you'll intentionally trigger yourself and use tVNS to recover. This is the core of the protocol.

#### Step 2A: Turn OFF tVNS

- **Reduce TENS intensity to ZERO**
- Keep electrodes on
- Keep bilateral audio playing (if using)

**Why turn it off?** You need to be able to access the trauma state. tVNS prevents that.

#### Step 2B: Target the Memory (2-5 minutes)

**Choose your target:**

- Start with a mildly distressing memory (3-4 out of 10)
- NOT your worst trauma (save that for later sessions)
- Something that makes you uncomfortable but not terrified

**Engage with the memory:**

1. Close your eyes
2. Bring up the memory vividly:
   - What do you see?
   - What do you hear?
   - What do you feel in your body?
   - What emotions arise?
3. **Stay with it** even as it gets uncomfortable
4. Let the bilateral audio keep you from dissociating

**Notice your nervous system response:**

- Heart rate increases
- Breathing shallows or quickens
- Chest tightness
- Jaw/shoulder tension
- Emotional intensity (fear, anger, shame, grief)
- Impulse to escape/avoid

#### Step 2C: Recognize Dysregulation

**You're looking for:**

- ✅ Emotional intensity (crying, anger, fear)
- ✅ Body activation (tension, shaking, heat)
- ✅ Increased distress
- ✅ Difficulty staying present

**BUT NOT:**

- ❌ Complete dissociation (feeling nothing, leaving your body)
- ❌ Overwhelm (can't breathe, panic attack)
- ❌ Re-traumatization (worse than when you started)

**Sweet spot:** Uncomfortable but not unbearable. If it's too much, turn tVNS back on immediately.

#### Step 2D: Hold Until Peak (1-3 minutes)

- Stay with the memory until distress peaks
- This might be 30 seconds or 3 minutes
- You'll know it's peaked when:
  - Intensity plateaus
  - You start to feel overwhelmed
  - Strong impulse to escape
  - Body maxes out tension

**Don't push past overwhelm.** If you hit 8-9/10 distress, go to next step.

#### Step 2E: Turn ON tVNS (Reset)

**Action:** Increase TENS intensity back to your baseline level.

**What should happen (30 seconds to 3 minutes):**

1. **Immediate body response**
   - Vagus nerve activation begins
   - Parasympathetic system engages

2. **Gradual regulation**
   - Heart rate starts to slow
   - Breathing deepens
   - Muscles begin to release
   - Emotional intensity reduces

3. **Return to window of tolerance**
   - You feel yourself "landing" back in your body
   - Present moment awareness returns
   - The memory loses its charge
   - You can think about it without as much distress

**This is the magic:** The memory is still there, but your nervous system isn't hijacked by it anymore.

#### Step 2F: Integration (2-5 minutes)

**Keep tVNS on for a few more minutes:**

- Let your system fully settle
- Notice what changed about the memory
- Journal if helpful (voice record or write)
- Common experiences:
  - "It feels further away now"
  - "I can see it without being in it"
  - "The charge is gone"
  - "I feel sad but not overwhelmed"

### Phase 3: Repeat Cycles (20-40 minutes total)

#### Option A: Same memory, multiple passes

- Go through Steps 2A-2F again with the same memory
- Each pass should be slightly less intense
- Stop when the memory no longer triggers dysregulation
- Typically 3-5 passes to fully process a mild memory

#### Option B: Multiple memories, single pass each

- Process 3-4 different memories in one session
- Only do 1-2 cycles per memory
- Come back to them in future sessions

**How many cycles in one session?**

- Beginners: 2-3 cycles
- Experienced: 4-6 cycles
- Total session time: 30-60 minutes max

## 🧠 Understanding What's Happening

### The Neuroscience

1. **Trauma Activation (tVNS OFF)**
   - You voluntarily enter Default Mode Network (DMN)
   - Trauma memory activates amygdala
   - Fight/flight/freeze response triggers
   - Sympathetic nervous system dominant

2. **Mechanical Intervention (tVNS ON)**
   - Vagus nerve stimulation activates parasympathetic system
   - Amygdala activity suppressed
   - Salience Network shifts you to Task Positive Network
   - You can "witness" the memory without being hijacked

3. **Memory Reconsolidation**
   - Trauma memory recalled in a safe state
   - Brain re-files the memory with new somatic tags
   - "This happened, but I'm safe now"
   - Emotional charge reduces with each pass

### Why Bilateral Stimulation Helps

**EMDR Theory:**

- Taxing working memory prevents dissociation
- Interhemispheric communication integrates split experience
- Mimics REM sleep processing
- Keeps you in Task Positive Network during memory work

## 📊 Tracking Your Progress

### Signs of Successful Processing

**During session:**

- ✅ Can access difficult memories
- ✅ Experience emotional intensity without overwhelm
- ✅ Notice body sensations shifting
- ✅ tVNS brings you back to baseline reliably
- ✅ Memory charge decreases with each cycle

**After session:**

- ✅ Feel tired but relieved (not depleted)
- ✅ May have insights or "aha" moments
- ✅ Memory feels more distant or neutral
- ✅ Sleep better that night
- ✅ Less triggered by related stimuli in daily life

**Over weeks/months:**

- ✅ Specific memories lose their charge permanently
- ✅ Fewer intrusive thoughts
- ✅ Less anxiety/hypervigilance
- ✅ More present in daily life
- ✅ Can discuss trauma without activation

### Signs to Slow Down or Stop

**During session:**

- ❌ Complete dissociation (numbness, leaving body)
- ❌ Panic attack or severe overwhelm
- ❌ tVNS doesn't bring you back to baseline
- ❌ Feeling worse instead of better

**After session:**

- ❌ Nightmares or increased flashbacks
- ❌ More triggered than before
- ❌ Can't sleep or concentrate
- ❌ Feeling re-traumatized

**If this happens:**

- Take a break from trauma processing
- Go back to just using tVNS for regulation (no memory work)
- Consider working with a therapist
- Try shorter, gentler sessions
- Start with less intense memories

## 🎯 Tips for Success

### For Beginners

1. **Start slow**
   - First session: Just tVNS, no trauma work (learn baseline)
   - Second session: One mild memory, one cycle
   - Build up gradually

2. **Choose targets carefully**
   - Start with 3-4/10 intensity memories
   - NOT your worst trauma
   - Save heavy stuff for when you're experienced

3. **Have support**
   - Tell someone you trust what you're doing
   - Have them check in after sessions
   - Consider having them in next room for first few times

4. **Journal after**
   - What memory you worked on
   - How many cycles
   - What shifted
   - How you feel

### Common Mistakes

❌ **Going too deep too fast**

- Processing your worst trauma in first session
- Hitting 9-10/10 distress regularly
- Multiple intense memories in one session

❌ **Not establishing baseline first**

- Jumping straight into trauma work
- Skipping the initial regulation phase
- Not learning what "safe" feels like

❌ **Inconsistent practice**

- One session then nothing for weeks
- Not giving memories time to reconsolidate
- Expecting instant results

❌ **Ignoring body signals**

- Pushing through severe overwhelm
- Ignoring dissociation
- Not stopping when dysregulated

### Best Practices

✅ **Session frequency:**

- 2-3 times per week optimal
- Space sessions 2-3 days apart
- Give nervous system time to integrate

✅ **Session duration:**

- 30-45 minutes typical
- Don't go longer than 60 minutes
- Multiple short sessions better than one marathon

✅ **Environment:**

- Same place each time (builds safety)
- Minimal distractions
- Comfortable temperature
- Soft lighting

✅ **After-care:**

- Gentle movement (walk, stretch)
- Hydrate well
- Avoid alcohol/substances same day
- Early bedtime (processing continues in sleep)

## 🧪 Advanced Techniques

### For Experienced Users

#### Progressive Desensitization

Once you're comfortable with mild memories:

1. Create a "hierarchy" of memories (3/10 to 9/10)
2. Fully process each level before moving up
3. Only move to next level when previous level is neutral
4. Typically takes weeks/months to reach worst memories

#### Extended Exposure

For particularly sticky memories:

- Longer activation phase (5-10 minutes instead of 2-3)
- Multiple cycles in one session (5-7 instead of 2-3)
- Daily sessions for one specific memory
- More intense but faster processing

#### No-Audio Sessions

Once skilled at staying present:

- Try processing without bilateral stimulation
- Tests your ability to hold difficult states
- Some people prefer the simplicity
- Higher skill floor required

#### Adding Body Work

Combine with somatic techniques:

- Tension release exercises during processing
- Vocalization (screaming into pillow, etc.)
- Physical movement between cycles
- Butterfly hug or bilateral tapping

1. **Your dysregulation patterns**

   - Before you dissociate completely
   - At peak distress (but not overwhelm)
   - When you feel "stuck" in the loop

   - What baseline feels like

## ❓ FAQ

### Q: How is this different from just using TENS for pain?

**A:** Standard TENS is for masking pain signals. This protocol:

- Uses specific vagus nerve targets (ear)
- Uses it as a nervous system regulator, not pain relief
- Combines with trauma processing (not just passive stimulation)
- Leverages Polyvagal Theory mechanisms

### Q: Can I do this daily?

**A:** 2-3 times per week is optimal. Daily sessions:

- Risk overworking your nervous system
- Don't allow integration time
- Can lead to habituation (reduced effectiveness)

### Q: How long until I see results?

**A:** Varies widely:

- **Immediate:** Feel regulation during/after sessions
- **1-2 weeks:** Specific memories lose charge
- **1-3 months:** Noticeable reduction in triggers
- **6-12 months:** Significant trauma resolution

### Q: What if I can't afford headphones?

**A:** Start without bilateral audio:

- tVNS alone still provides nervous system regulation
- You can process trauma in silence
- Add audio later when budget allows
- Or use free YouTube EMDR tracks with cheap earbuds

### Q: Can I use this for anxiety/panic (not trauma)?

**A:** Yes! tVNS alone (without memory work) is excellent for:

- Acute anxiety
- Panic attacks
- Hypervigilance
- Insomnia
- Just turn it on, no memory targeting needed

### Q: What if I dissociate and can't come back?

**Emergency protocol:**

1. Turn tVNS to maximum comfortable intensity
2. Remove headphones
3. Open eyes
4. 5-4-3-2-1 grounding (5 things you see, 4 you hear, etc.)
5. Stand up and move your body
6. Call your support person

**Prevention:** Always have someone within calling distance for first 5-10 sessions.

### Q: Can I do this drunk/high?

**A: NO.** Never use this protocol:

- Under influence of alcohol
- After using cannabis/drugs
- On sedatives or sleep meds
- When judgment is impaired

Wait 24 hours after substance use.

### Q: Should I tell my therapist?

**A: Yes!** This complements therapy:

- Ideally work with a trauma-informed therapist
- They can help you choose targets
- Provide support if you hit difficult material
- Validate your progress

This is NOT a replacement for therapy - it's a tool to support therapeutic work.
